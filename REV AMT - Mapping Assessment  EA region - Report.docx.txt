The assessment was undertaken to map youth-led civil society organizations (CSOs), analyze emerging thematic and legal advocacy priorities, examine institutional and operational trends, and generate insight into the evolving landscape of youth-led organizing within Eastern Africa.The review focused specifically on organizations from Tanzania, Kenya, Uganda, and Ethiopia. However, the consolidated dataset provided for analysis reflected significant disparities in regional participation and response representation. Tanzania emerged as the only country with extractable and analyzable organizational data within the reviewed dataset, while no complete extractable entries were identified for Kenya, Uganda, or Ethiopia at the time of analysis. Consequently, the findings presented within this report are primarily informed by Tanzanian organizational responses and should therefore be interpreted within the context of uneven regional data representation.Despite these methodological limitations, the Tanzania findings provide important insight into broader youth-led organizing dynamics shaping Eastern Africa. The assessment reveals an evolving youth-led civil society ecosystem characterized by strong grassroots engagement, rights-based programming, community-centered advocacy, increasing climate justice engagement, and growing youth participation within local accountability and civic processes.The findings demonstrate that youth-led organizations are increasingly occupying critical advocacy and community engagement spaces traditionally dominated by larger and more institutionalized civil society actors. Organizations reflected strong engagement in areas including gender justice, prevention of violence against women and girls, sexual and reproductive health and rights (SRHR), climate justice, youth civic participation, economic empowerment, and community accountability initiatives.A significant trend emerging from the assessment is the growing adoption of integrated and intersectional advocacy approaches. Many youth-led organizations increasingly recognize the interconnected relationship between gender inequality, economic vulnerability, climate insecurity, educational exclusion, and youth marginalization. As a result, organizations are progressively adopting more holistic and community-responsive programming models aimed at addressing multiple dimensions of social inequality simultaneously.The findings further highlight the strong grassroots orientation of many youth-led organizations. Unlike larger national organizations that often operate through highly institutionalized structures, many youth-led initiatives appear deeply embedded within local communities and maintain close engagement with affected populations. This positioning strengthens community trust, accessibility, and responsiveness while also reinforcing the role of youth-led organizations as important actors within local advocacy and social transformation processes.At the same time, the assessment reveals persistent structural and operational barriers affecting the sustainability and long-term effectiveness of youth-led organizations across the region. Key challenges identified include financial insecurity, limited institutional development, inadequate legal advocacy capacity, fragmented regional coordination mechanisms, and limited access to formal policy and decision-making spaces. Many organizations continue to operate within resource-constrained environments characterized by short-term project funding, reliance on volunteerism, and limited institutional support.The assessment therefore underscores the need for increased investment in strengthening youth-led ecosystems across Eastern Africa. This includes strengthening institutional capacity, expanding access to sustainable and flexible funding mechanisms, improving regional coordination and movement-building, supporting legal advocacy and policy engagement, and creating more inclusive pathways for youth participation within governance and decision-making processes.Overall, the report concludes that youth-led organizations across Eastern Africa continue to demonstrate significant resilience, innovation, community legitimacy, and commitment to social transformation despite operating within challenging institutional and financial environments. With targeted investment and sustained support, youth-led organizations hold substantial potential to strengthen civic participation, advance rights-based advocacy, and contribute meaningfully to inclusive and sustainable development across the region.Background and Regional ContextAcross Eastern and Southern Africa, young people continue to constitute a significant proportion of the region’s population and remain central to ongoing social, political, economic, and environmental transformations. Despite their demographic significance and growing influence within civic spaces, many young people continue to face persistent structural challenges including unemployment, gender inequality, shrinking civic space, climate vulnerability, exclusion from decision-making processes, and limited access to essential services and opportunities.In response to these realities, youth-led civil society organizations have increasingly emerged as important actors within regional advocacy, governance, and development landscapes. Unlike more traditional and highly institutionalized civil society structures, many youth-led organizations operate in close proximity to grassroots communities and are often deeply informed by lived experiences and local realities. This community-centered positioning has enabled youth-led organizations to play a growing role in advancing rights awareness, community mobilization, social accountability, gender justice, climate action, economic empowerment, and youth participation initiatives across the region.The increasing visibility and influence of youth-led organizations reflects a broader shift within regional advocacy ecosystems, where young people are no longer viewed solely as beneficiaries of development interventions but are increasingly positioning themselves as organizers, advocates, movement builders, and contributors to policy and social transformation processes.Within Eastern Africa specifically, youth-led advocacy has become particularly important in response to rising youth unemployment, gender-based violence, child marriage, educational exclusion, climate-related economic vulnerabilities, and continued concerns around democratic participation and youth representation within governance structures. Youth-led organizations are therefore increasingly occupying critical spaces in promoting inclusion, strengthening community resilience, and amplifying youth voices within local and national conversations around justice, equity, and accountability.This assessment contributes to broader regional efforts aimed at understanding the operational realities, advocacy priorities, institutional capacities, and emerging trends shaping youth-led organizing within Eastern Africa.Purpose and Objectives of the AssessmentThe overall purpose of the assessment was to map and analyze youth-led organizations operating within the ESA region and generate evidence on emerging advocacy and legal engagement trends.The specific objectives included:Identifying youth-led civil society organizations operating within Eastern Africa;Mapping organizational thematic priorities and areas of intervention;Assessing emerging legal and advocacy trends;Identifying regional legal and policy “hotspots” affecting youth constituencies;Understanding operational strengths and institutional gaps affecting youth-led organizing;Generating evidence-based recommendations to strengthen regional youth-led advocacy ecosystems.MethodologyThe assessment utilized a consolidated regional survey dataset containing submissions from youth-led organizations across the Eastern and Southern Africa (ESA) region. The analytical process focused specifically on extracting and reviewing responses linked to Tanzania, Kenya, Uganda, and Ethiopia in line with the scope of the Eastern Africa regional mapping assignment.The methodology adopted a desk-based qualitative and thematic analysis approach aimed at identifying emerging patterns, advocacy priorities, institutional trends, and operational realities shaping youth-led organizing within the region. The assessment involved a systematic review of organizational entries within the dataset, including country-specific filtering, extraction of relevant organizational profiles, and thematic coding of responses.Particular attention was given to understanding the nature of youth-led advocacy, legal engagement approaches, community-focused interventions, and organizational sustainability challenges reflected within the responses. The analysis further examined recurring trends related to organizational thematic focus areas, target populations, advocacy priorities, institutional capacity, partnerships and networks, operational constraints, and funding sustainability concerns.In addition, the assessment incorporated comparative review of the available regional representation in order to identify patterns in participation, data availability, and visibility of youth-led organizations across the targeted Eastern African countries.The methodological approach was designed to generate both quantitative mapping insights and qualitative narrative analysis capable of capturing the evolving landscape of youth-led organizing, rights-based advocacy, and community engagement within Eastern Africa.Data Availability and Assessment LimitationsA key methodological observation emerging from the assessment relates to uneven data representation across the targeted Eastern African countries. The findings suggest that youth-led organizations are increasingly positioning themselves not only as service providers, but also as important actors in shaping community dialogue, strengthening civic engagement, and promoting social transformation at local level.A recurring trend emerging from the assessment is the strong rights-based orientation reflected across many youth-led initiatives. Organizations demonstrated growing commitment to promoting inclusion, equity, participation, and community resilience through advocacy approaches grounded in local realities and lived experiences. Many organizations appear to prioritize direct engagement with communities through awareness campaigns, peer education, community forums, grassroots organizing, and youth-centered mobilization efforts.The findings further indicate that youth-led organizations in Tanzania are increasingly functioning as important platforms for youth agency and collective action. Through their work, these organizations are contributing to stronger community participation, increased awareness of social justice concerns, and greater youth engagement within local accountability and governance processes.An important observation emerging from the dataset is the strong grassroots orientation of many youth-led organizations. Unlike larger and highly institutionalized national organizations, many youth-led initiatives appear deeply embedded within local communities and maintain close proximity to the populations they serve. This community-centered positioning enables organizations to respond more directly to emerging local challenges and strengthens trust, accessibility, and community ownership within their interventions.At the same time, the findings suggest that this grassroots orientation also presents certain institutional limitations. While close community engagement strengthens legitimacy and responsiveness, many organizations continue to experience challenges related to institutional visibility, long-term sustainability, technical capacity, and access to funding opportunities. The findings indicate that many youth-led organizations are actively engaged in advocacy and community interventions aimed at addressing violence against women and girls, harmful social norms, child marriage, educational exclusion, and barriers affecting adolescent girls and young women.Organizations demonstrated strong commitment to promoting gender justice through community sensitization, awareness campaigns, peer engagement, youth forums, and educational initiatives focused on strengthening protection and empowerment for girls and young women. Many organizations also integrated menstrual health awareness and support for adolescent girls within broader gender equality programming.The prominence of gender-based violence prevention within the findings reflects the continuing urgency of gender inequality issues affecting communities across Eastern Africa. The assessment further suggests that youth-led organizations increasingly recognize the relationship between gender justice, economic vulnerability, educational access, and civic participation.Rather than relying heavily on litigation-oriented approaches, most organizations appear to prioritize prevention-focused and community-centered advocacy strategies aimed at shifting harmful attitudes, increasing awareness, and strengthening local accountability. Youth-led organizations demonstrated growing engagement in SRHR awareness, menstrual health and hygiene advocacy, youth health education, prevention of teenage pregnancy, and broader conversations around bodily autonomy and reproductive rights.The findings reflect broader regional realities where many adolescents and young people continue to face barriers in accessing accurate information, youth-friendly health services, and safe spaces for engagement on sexual and reproductive health issues. In response, youth-led organizations are increasingly positioning themselves as important platforms for awareness creation, peer education, and community dialogue around SRHR-related concerns.The assessment further indicates that SRHR advocacy is often closely linked to broader gender justice and protection efforts. Many organizations appear to understand the relationship between reproductive health, educational retention for girls, prevention of child marriage, reduction of exploitation, and broader efforts aimed at addressing stigma and discrimination affecting young people.Youth-led organizations therefore continue to occupy important spaces within communities where public systems and institutional support mechanisms remain insufficient, particularly in relation to youth-centered engagement and community-level awareness creation.6.3 Climate Justice and Environmental AdvocacyAn important emerging trend identified throughout the assessment is the increasing integration of climate justice and environmental advocacy within youth-led programming and organizing. In many cases, organizations are compelled to prioritize short-term project delivery over strategic organizational growth and long-term movement-building efforts.The assessment further suggests that current funding models often prioritize programmatic outputs while overlooking the institutional realities and operational needs of youth-led organizations. Limited investment in core organizational systems, leadership development, staff wellbeing, governance strengthening, and operational infrastructure continues to constrain the sustainability and effectiveness of youth-led organizing within the region.At the same time, the findings highlight important opportunities for development partners, regional institutions, philanthropic actors, and funding mechanisms to adopt more inclusive and youth-centered financing approaches capable of strengthening long-term organizational resilience and advocacy influence.Key opportunities identified include:Expanding flexible grant-making models responsive to grassroots realities;Increasing access to core institutional and operational support funding;Promoting long-term partnership frameworks rather than short-term transactional funding relationships;Simplifying application, compliance, and reporting processes for youth-led organizations;Integrating institutional capacity strengthening alongside programmatic financingSuch approaches would enable youth-led organizations to transition from survival-oriented implementation models toward stronger institutional sustainability, strategic growth, and long-term advocacy engagement.Importantly, the assessment emphasizes that investing in youth-led organizations should not be understood solely as support for isolated projects or activities. Rather, such investment represents a strategic contribution toward strengthening civic leadership, democratic participation, grassroots accountability, and community resilience across Eastern Africa.Strengthening youth-centered funding ecosystems therefore remains essential to ensuring that youth-led organizations are not only able to sustain their operations, but are also empowered to influence policy processes, strengthen community agency, and contribute meaningfully to long-term social transformation within the region.10.4 Climate Justice IntegrationThe assessment also reveals campaigns;Green entrepreneurship models;Climate resilience programming;Youth-centered environmental advocacy.Strengthening climate justice integration within youth-led ecosystems could therefore populations around climate action while simultaneously linking environmental advocacy with broader conversations around equity, justice, livelihoods, and human rights.Ultimately, the growing youth-led organizations across Eastern Africa. While youth-led organizations continue to play an increasingly important role in advancing social justice, civic engagement, gender equality, climate action, and community accountability, the findings also highlight the need for more intentional and sustained investment in strengthening youth-led ecosystems within the region.The following recommendations are proposed to strengthen the sustainability, visibility, institutional resilience, and advocacy influence of youth-led organizations across Eastern Africa.11.1 Strengthen Regional Coordination, Networking, and Movement-BuildingThe findings reveal a strong need to strengthen regional coordination and collaboration among youth-led organizations operating across Eastern Africa. Although many organizations are addressing interconnected social, economic, governance, and environmental challenges, youth-led organizing within the region remains highly fragmented, with limited opportunities for sustained cross-border engagement and collective advocacy.There is therefore a need to invest in stronger regional networking and solidarity mechanisms capable of:Facilitating cross-country collaboration and peer learning;Strengthening collective advocacy platforms and youth-led coalitions;Supporting regional movement-building initiatives;Enhancing knowledge exchange and mentorship opportunities;Amplifying youth-led voices within regional governance and policy spaces.Strengthened regional ecosystems would enable youth-led organizations to collectively address shared regional concerns including gender inequality, youth unemployment, shrinking civic space, climate vulnerability, and barriers to democratic participation.Regional coordination platforms could also contribute to stronger collective bargaining power, increased visibility, and more coordinated responses to emerging human rights and governance challenges affecting young people across the region.11.2 Increase Investments in Institutional Strengthening and Organizational DevelopmentThe assessment highlights the urgent need for increased investment in the institutional development and long-term sustainability of youth-led organizations.While many organizations demonstrate strong grassroots legitimacy, innovation, and commitment to community transformation, several continue to operate with limited governance systems, weak operational infrastructure, and inadequate organizational development support.Development partners, regional institutions, and philanthropic actors should therefore prioritize investments aimed at strengthening:Governance and accountability systems;Strategic planning and organizational development;Monitoring, evaluation, accountability, and learning frameworks;Documentation and knowledge management systems;Leadership development and succession planning;Financial management and operational systems.Institutional strengthening support should move beyond short-term project implementation and focus on building resilient organizations capable of sustaining long-term advocacy, community engagement, and movement-building efforts.Importantly, strengthening institutional systems within youth-led organizations is essential not only for improving operational effectiveness, but also for enhancing organizational credibility, sustainability, and ability to access larger funding and partnership opportunities.11.3 Expand Legal Advocacy and Policy Engagement CapacityThe findings demonstrate significant opportunities to strengthen legal advocacy, policy engagement, and rights-based programming among youth-led organizations.Although many organizations are actively addressing rights-related issues at community level, most advocacy efforts remain heavily centered on awareness creation, sensitization, and grassroots mobilization, with limited participation in formal legal and policy processes.There is therefore a need for targeted investments aimed at strengthening:Legal literacy and civic education;Human rights awareness and accountability advocacy;Policy analysis and legislative engagement;Strategic litigation and legal empowerment approaches;Evidence-based advocacy and research capacity;Youth participation in governance and accountability processes.Strengthening legal advocacy capacity would enable youth-led organizations to engage more effectively with policy actors, accountability systems, and governance institutions at local, national, and regional levels.Increased investment in legal empowerment would also strengthen the ability of youth-led organizations to bridge the gap between grassroots realities and formal policy processes, ensuring that youth voices and lived experiences are meaningfully reflected within governance and legal frameworks.11.4 Improve Access to Sustainable and Flexible Funding MechanismsThe assessment strongly emphasizes the need for more flexible, accessible, and youth-responsive funding approaches capable of supporting the long-term sustainability of youth-led organizations.Current funding models often remain inaccessible to smaller grassroots organizations due to complex compliance requirements, rigid donor conditions, short-term project cycles, and limited institutional support funding.Development partners and funding institutions should therefore adopt more inclusive financing approaches that:Expand access to flexible and simplified grant-making mechanisms;Increase availability of core operational and institutional support funding;Promote long-term partnership models rather than short-term transactional funding relationships;Simplify reporting and compliance processes for grassroots organizations;Integrate institutional strengthening alongside programmatic financing.More flexible funding ecosystems would allow youth-led organizations to move beyond survival-oriented programming and invest in sustainable institutional growth, leadership development, staff wellbeing, strategic advocacy, and long-term community impact.Importantly, supporting youth-led organizations should be recognized as a strategic investment in democratic participation, civic leadership, community resilience, and inclusive development across Eastern Africa.11.5 Strengthen Regional Data Collection, Research, and Knowledge Management SystemsThe assessment findings highlight important gaps in regional data availability and documentation relating to youth-led organizing within Eastern Africa. Limited participation from several target countries constrained the ability to conduct broader comparative regional analysis and reflects wider challenges associated with documentation, visibility, and knowledge management within youth-led ecosystems.There is therefore a need to strengthen:Regional mapping and documentation systems;Data collection methodologies and outreach strategies;Youth-led research and evidence-generation initiatives;Knowledge management and organizational documentation practices;Regional learning and information-sharing platforms.Improved data systems would support stronger evidence-based advocacy, increase visibility of youth-led organizations, and strengthen understanding of emerging regional trends affecting young people and grassroots movements.Strengthening research and documentation systems would also contribute to more inclusive regional assessments and ensure that youth-led organizations are better represented within policy discussions, funding priorities, and development planning processes.11.6 Support Intersectional and Integrated Programming ApproachesThe findings reveal increasing recognition among youth-led organizations that social inequalities affecting young people are deeply interconnected. Organizations are increasingly linking climate justice, gender equality, economic empowerment, SRHR, governance, and community resilience within their advocacy and programming approaches.Development actors and regional institutions should therefore support more intersectional and integrated programming models capable of responding to the complex realities affecting young people across the region.Particular attention should be given to strengthening programming that connects:Climate justice and economic resilience;Gender equality and governance participation;Youth livelihoods and social protection;SRHR and educational inclusion;Community resilience and sustainable development.Integrated programming approaches would strengthen the ability of youth-led organizations to address root causes of vulnerability while promoting more holistic and community-centered development responses.Such approaches are particularly important within contexts where young people experience overlapping forms of exclusion, inequality, and socio-economic vulnerability.11.7 Strengthen Youth Participation in Policy and Governance ProcessesThe assessment findings indicate that despite growing youth-led activism and grassroots engagement, many young people and youth-led organizations continue to face barriers in accessing formal policy and governance spaces.There is therefore a need for governments, regional institutions, civil society actors, and development partners to create stronger and more institutionalized pathways for youth participation within decision-making processes.Efforts should focus on:Increasing youth representation within policy dialogue and consultation mechanisms;Strengthening youth participation in governance and accountability structures;Supporting youth-led participation within regional advocacy forums;Creating more inclusive civic engagement spaces for young people;Strengthening partnerships between youth-led organizations and public institutions.Meaningful youth participation should move beyond symbolic engagement toward genuine inclusion within policy formulation, implementation, monitoring, and accountability processes.Strengthening youth participation in governance systems is critical to advancing democratic accountability, responsive leadership, and inclusive development across Eastern Africa.Ultimately, ensuring that youth-led organizations are recognized not merely as beneficiaries but as equal stakeholders within governance and development ecosystems remains essential to strengthening long-term social transformation within the region.ConclusionYouth-led organizations continue to play an increasingly important role in shaping advocacy, social justice, and community transformation across Eastern Africa. Although the available dataset reflected limited representation from Kenya, Uganda, and Ethiopia, the findings from Tanzania provide important insight into the evolving landscape of youth-led organizing within the region.The assessment demonstrates that youth-led organizations are increasingly contributing to advocacy and community engagement efforts related to gender justice, sexual and reproductive health and rights (SRHR), climate action, civic participation, economic empowerment, and community accountability. Through grassroots mobilization and rights-based programming, these organizations continue to strengthen community awareness, youth participation, and local responses to emerging social challenges.The findings further highlight both the resilience and the structural vulnerabilities affecting youth-led organizations across the region. On one hand, many organizations demonstrate strong community legitimacy, innovation, adaptability, and commitment to addressing urgent social and development concerns affecting young people and marginalized communities. On the other hand, persistent barriers including financial insecurity, limited institutional development, inadequate technical capacity, and restricted access to policy and decision-making spaces continue to affect organizational sustainability and long-term influence.The assessment therefore underscores the importance of strengthening youth-led ecosystems through sustained investment in institutional development, leadership strengthening, legal advocacy capacity, regional coordination, and more accessible and flexible funding mechanisms. Strengthening these areas remains essential to enhancing the effectiveness, sustainability, and policy engagement capacity of youth-led organizations across Eastern Africa.Ultimately, the findings reflect the growing significance of youth-led organizations as important actors within regional advocacy, governance, and community transformation processes. Their continued presence within grassroots spaces highlights the critical role of youth leadership in advancing inclusive participation, rights-based development, and locally driven social change across the region.
