Executive SummaryThe two-day Youth-Led Digital Advocacy Convening brought together 15 youth advocates (3 males, 12 females) from diverse backgrounds, including persons with disabilities and adolescents, to strengthen regional capacity for ending child marriage through digital advocacy and movement building. The workshop focused on building technical mastery of the SADC Model Law on Eradicating Child Marriage and Protecting Children Already in Marriage, while co-designing a regional digital advocacy campaign.Key outcomes included the draft development of an advocacy strategy, the establishment of a WhatsApp platform for ongoing engagement, and the identification of critical entry points for effective youth-led advocacy using national and regional policy frameworks.2. Introduction and BackgroundChild marriage remains a critical challenge across the SADC region. Zambia has made significant legislative progress through the Children's Code Act No. 12 of 2022 and the Marriage (Amendment) Act No. 13 of 2023, which set the minimum age of marriage at 18 years without exceptions. Despite these legal reforms, implementation gaps persist, particularly regarding protection pathways for children already in marriage, school re-entry policies, and the economic vulnerability of young brides.The Pan African Alliance Ending Child Marriage (PAAECM) convened this hybrid workshop to address these gaps by equipping youth advocates with technical knowledge of the SADC Model Law and digital advocacy skills to accelerate progress. The workshop recognized that young people must take centre stage in advocacy work, ensuring that cross-cutting issues such as SGBV, HIV and AIDS, and climate change are incorporated into movement-building efforts.3. Workshop ObjectivesThe convening aimed to:Build shared technical mastery of the SADC Model Law on Eradicating Child Marriage and Protecting Children Already in MarriageStrengthen youth-led movement building and digital advocacy capacity to accelerate progress towards ending child marriageCo-design a regional digital advocacy campaign linking Model Law asks with movement voicesEstablish sustainable platforms for ongoing youth engagement and collaboration across the region4. ConclusionThe Youth-Led Digital Advocacy Convening successfully brought together diverse youth advocates to build technical mastery of the SADC Model Law and strengthen digital advocacy capacity for ending child marriage. The workshop demonstrated that inclusivity, participatory approaches, and policy-aligned advocacy are critical for effective movement building. While challenges such as limited resources, implementation gaps, and capacity constraints remain, the draft advocacy strategy, established WhatsApp platform, and 6-month commitments provide a strong foundation for sustained action.The convening reinforced that young people must lead advocacy efforts, ensuring that cross-cutting issues such as SGBV, HIV and AIDS, and climate change are integrated into all work to end child marriage. With ongoing mentorship, resource mobilization, and strategic engagement of influencers and policymakers, the youth-led movement is well-positioned to accelerate progress towards ending child marriage across the SADC region.
